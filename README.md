Blood-dependent organism stats use continuous formulas rather than lookup tables. The stronger blood-volume curve begins compensation, pulse loss, and oxygen-delivery loss earlier during active hemorrhage; tachycardia becomes severe through 3500-2000 mL before preload failure drives terminal hypotension. Bleeding alone does not consume the tissue O2 reserve: hemorrhagic O2 loss begins only as its resulting palpable pulse and delivery fail. Sustained severe hypovolemia also develops a smooth probabilistic cardiac-collapse hazard instead of using a lethal cutoff: the meaningful danger band begins near 3000 mL, worsens through 2500 mL, and becomes dangerous around 2000 mL. Hypotension lowers consciousness to a severe but non-OTRUB floor, while the resulting cerebral oxygen loss owns unconsciousness and anoxic brain injury. Tissue O2 is coupled to perfusion and causes stamina, movement, disorientation, and fake-up impairment without directly changing consciousness or requesting OTRUB. Correlated respiratory and circulation limits use the weakest current reserve once instead of multiplying pulse, pressure, perfusion, lung damage, and O2 into repeated penalties.
