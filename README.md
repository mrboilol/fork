Blood-dependent organism stats use continuous formulas rather than lookup tables. Circulating volume continuously limits pressure, oxygen capacity, perfusion, pulse, and cardiac output, reaching zero only with zero blood. Minor blood loss stays below the symptom dead zone, while hemorrhage compensation rises sharply through 3500-2000 mL, peaks near 300 BPM around 2000-1750 mL, and then falls as preload fails. Low circulating volume, active bleeding, and low palpable pulse also deplete the O2 reserve instead of leaving it at full saturation. Sustained severe hypovolemia also develops a smooth probabilistic cardiac-collapse hazard instead of using a lethal cutoff: uncommon complications begin through the upper danger band, risk rises noticeably between 3000 and 2750 mL, and then accelerates sharply toward very common collapse at deep blood loss. Hypotension lowers consciousness to a severe but non-OTRUB floor, while the resulting cerebral oxygen loss owns unconsciousness and anoxic brain injury. Tissue O2 is coupled to perfusion and causes stamina, movement, disorientation, and fake-up impairment without directly changing consciousness or requesting OTRUB. Correlated respiratory and circulation limits use the weakest current reserve once instead of multiplying pulse, pressure, perfusion, lung damage, and O2 into repeated penalties. Exertion O2 debt and the internal-bleed injury score never create systemic ischemia by themselves; ischemia requires pathological hypoxemia, actual severe blood loss, or transfusion shock.

The compatible Judge/Vottur physiology additions stay inside those existing owners: live mean/systolic/diastolic pressure, cardiac output and stroke volume are calculated in the pulse module; venous, arterial, and internal bleed rates plus cerebral/cardiac status are replicated to organism statistics; and stamina regeneration multipliers applied by shields or melee recover over time.
